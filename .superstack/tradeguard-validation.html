TradeGuard Pro: Validation and Competitive Landscape

An MT5 rule-enforcement SaaS running on MetaApi · research date 2026-09-28

Verdict: PIVOT to a narrow first product (wedge) · confidence 0.5

The problem is real and people pay for it. But the two directions proposed earlier (“prop firm in a box” and “on-chain verified track records”) both run into well-funded incumbents, and one of those is MetaApi itself. Don't build either as the core product. Run a one-week validation sprint on a narrower wedge: rule enforcement plus a mentor dashboard for trading educators and signal communities. That segment is underserved, it's a B2B2C sale, and none of the competitors below target it.

2 · Demand signals

Strong: people pay money for this category

  • White-label prop platforms cost $5k–50k to set up plus $2k–15k/mo, and $1–10 per account (Track360). Prop firms spend real budget on risk engines.
  • At least two paid B2C “behavioral firewall” products are live: LockMyTrades at $29.99/mo and TradeLock. Traders pay to have their own rules enforced (LockMyTrades, TradeLock).
  • Traders build manual workarounds. There are long-running MQL5 and ForexFactory threads asking how to lock an account after a loss (MQL5, ForexFactory).
  • Prop firm crypto payouts reached $115M in Q1 2026, up 129% year on year, and 49% of firms now offer them (PropFirmMap).

Missing: evidence for TradeGuard itself

  • No user interviews, waitlist, letters of intent (LOIs) or paying pilots. Per the rubric, the next step is to validate, not to build.

Dead projects and landmines

  • 80–100 prop firms shut down (13–14% of the industry) after MetaQuotes started revoking MT4/MT5 licenses in Feb 2024. Firms now reportedly need a forex/CFD license plus a bank reference letter to keep MT5 (PropFirmGuide, Cointracts).
  • US-facing firms left MetaTrader almost entirely. More than 40 firms, including FTMO, now license DXtrade (Thor). A product that only supports MT5 has a shrinking market in the US.

Moat

The most realistic moat is distribution and lock-in: once an educator's cohort, rule templates and history live in TradeGuard, switching costs them effort. A data moat (patterns of behavior across thousands of accounts) builds up slowly on top of that. Technical complexity is not a moat, because MetaApi exposes the same primitives to anyone.

4 · Crypto necessity: “what breaks without the blockchain?”

  • Core enforcement: nothing breaks. Crypto isn't needed here.
  • Verified track records: they only partly work. The data comes from MT5 through MetaApi, so an on-chain hash proves TradeGuard said so, not that the trades happened. That's an oracle problem: the trust moves to you instead of going away. A signed attestation (for example through the Solana Attestation Service) is still useful for tamper-evidence and portability, but it's a feature, not the product.
  • USDC payouts on Solana: this is a real improvement over Arbitrum-based Rise for traders in emerging markets, but it's a separate business.

Conclusion: build TradeGuard as a Web2 SaaS. Add Solana for attestations and payouts only once there are paying users who ask for them.

6 · Pivot options

  • Recommended: narrower first product (wedge pivot). Same engine, sold to trading educators, mentors and signal communities. Rule templates per cohort, a compliance leaderboard, breach alerts in Telegram and Discord, and a mentor dashboard. Priced per seat. The educator sells it to their students for you.
  • Different user segment (adjacent pivot). A compliance layer for small prop firms that already run on white-label platforms, where TradeGuard's rules are richer than their built-in risk engine. This means a harder sales process.
  • Different layer (stack pivot). A multi-platform rule engine API covering MT5, cTrader, DXtrade and Match-Trader. This removes the dependency on MetaApi and MetaQuotes.

7 · Build it yourself or integrate?

  • Drawdown and equity tracking: use MetaApi's Risk Management API rather than building your own trackers. Put your own logic only into the rules and the UX.
  • Attestations (later): use the Solana Attestation Service. No custom program is needed.
  • Payouts (later): integrate an existing USDC rail. Don't build a custodial payout program.

8 · One-week validation sprint

  1. Days 1–2: list 30 trading educators and signal-group owners with more than 1k members (Telegram, Discord, X, YouTube), focusing on markets where MT5 still dominates. DM 20 of them.
  2. Days 2–4: hold 8–10 calls. Ask: “How do you know whether your students follow your risk rules? What happens when they blow accounts?” Don't pitch.
  3. Day 3: put up a landing page with a real price (for example, $99/mo for 25 seats) and a “Start pilot” button.
  4. Days 5–7: offer 3 paid pilots. Also interview 10 funded traders to find out why they would or wouldn't choose TradeGuard over LockMyTrades.

Pass bar: at least 2 paid pilots or signed LOIs, plus at least 5 of 10 educators describing the problem unprompted. Kill bar: no pilots after 20 conversations. If that happens, fall back to the multi-platform rule engine API (stack pivot).
